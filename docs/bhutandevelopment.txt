Bhutan is a landlocked country in the eastern Himalayas, located between India to the south and the Tibet Autonomous Region of China to the north. It covers about 38,000 square kilometers and has a population of fewer than one million people. The capital and largest city is Thimphu.

The landscape of Bhutan changes dramatically from south to north. The southern foothills are subtropical, with warm weather and dense forests. The central valleys, such as Paro, Punakha, and Bumthang, have a temperate climate. The northern region is covered with high peaks and glaciers. Gangkhar Puensum, at about 7,570 meters, is one of the highest unclimbed mountains in the world, because Bhutan prohibits climbing mountains of spiritual importance.

Bhutan is famous for protecting its environment. The constitution requires that at least sixty percent of the country remain under forest cover. Large areas of the country are national parks and protected reserves. Wildlife includes the Bengal tiger, snow leopard, red panda, and the takin, which is the national animal. The Phobjikha valley is a winter home to endangered black-necked cranes.

Bhutan is a constitutional monarchy. The country moved from absolute monarchy to democracy in 2008, when the first national elections were held. The current king is Jigme Khesar Namgyel Wangchuck, who is known as the Fifth King. Bhutan is divided into twenty districts called dzongkhags.

Bhutan is well known for the philosophy of Gross National Happiness. This idea was promoted by the Fourth King, Jigme Singye Wangchuck, who said that the happiness of the people is more important than economic output alone. Gross National Happiness is based on four main pillars: sustainable development, preservation of culture, protection of the environment, and good governance.

The economy of Bhutan depends heavily on hydropower. Rivers flowing down from the Himalayas are used to produce electricity, and much of it is sold to India. Tourism, agriculture, and small industries are also important. The currency is the ngultrum, which is linked in value to the Indian rupee.

Bhutan manages tourism carefully to protect its culture and environment. Most visitors pay a daily Sustainable Development Fee, which supports local communities and conservation. Bhutan only opened to foreign tourists in 1974, and it introduced television and the internet as late as 1999.

The main international airport is in Paro, and the national airline is Drukair. Roads between towns are narrow and winding, so travel across the mountains takes much longer than the distances suggest.
